# Lesson 6.3: Word Stress & Sentence Rhythm: Sounding Natural

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 6.3
- **Core Concept:** Mastering Word Stress (syllable emphasis) and Sentence Rhythm (stress-timing) to sound clear, confident, and native-like.
- **Learning Objective:** By the end of this lesson, you will correctly stress key syllables in multi-syllable words and apply the "Music of English" (stressing content words, reducing function words) in full sentences to improve intelligibility and flow.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### A. Word Stress: The "Anchor" of the Word
English words have **one primary stressed syllable**. It is **LOUDER, LONGER, and HIGHER in pitch**. Unstressed syllables are fast, quiet, and often use the **schwa sound (/ə/)**.
*   **Rule of Thumb:** Nouns/Adjectives often stress the **1st syllable** (`PRE-sent`, `OB-ject`). Verbs often stress the **2nd syllable** (`pre-SENT`, `ob-JECT`).
*   **Suffix Rules:** Suffixes like `-tion`, `-sion`, `-ic`, `-ity`, `-logy` pull stress to the syllable *before* them (`in-for-MA-tion`, `e-CON-o-mic`, `a-BIL-i-ty`).

### B. Sentence Rhythm: Stress-Timing (The "Morse Code" Effect)
English is **stress-timed**, not syllable-timed. We spend equal time between **stressed beats**, crushing the unstressed words in between.
*   **Content Words (Stressed):** Nouns, Main Verbs, Adjectives, Adverbs, Negatives (`not`, `never`), Question Words (`who`, `what`), Demonstratives (`this`, `that`).
*   **Function Words (Unstressed/Reduced):** Articles (`a`, `the`), Prepositions (`to`, `for`, `at`), Pronouns (`he`, `she`, `it`), Auxiliary Verbs (`am`, `is`, `are`, `have`, `do`), Conjunctions (`and`, `but`).

### C. High-Frequency Spoken Formulas (Reduction Patterns)
| Written Form | Spoken Rhythm (IPA approx.) | Key Reduction |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I want to go** | /aɪ wɑnə goʊ/ | "want to" → **wanna** |
| **Going to** | /gʌnə/ | "going to" → **gonna** |
| **Have to / Has to** | /hæftə/ / hæstə/ | "have" → **hafta** / **hasta** |
| **What do you** | /wʌdʒə/ | "do you" → **d'ya** / **ja** |
| **Did you** | /dɪdʒə/ | "did you" → **didja** |
| **To the** | /təðə/ | "to" → **t'** (flap T) |

**When/Why to use:** Use these reductions **always** in natural speech (meetings, calls, coffee chats). Avoiding them makes you sound robotic, stiff, or angry. Clarity comes from *contrasting* the big words with the small, fast words.

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

| # | Context | ❌ Flat / Syllable-Timed (Robotic) | ✅ Natural Stress & Rhythm (Clear) | Why the ✅ Works |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Project Update** | "I will send the report to you tomorrow." (Every word equal weight) | "**I'll send the report** t' **you to-MOR-row**." | Stresses **send, report, tomorrow**. Reduces *will→'ll, to→t', you→ya*. |
| **2** | **Clarifying a Task** | "What do you think about the new design?" | "**What d'ya think** 'bout the **new design**?" | **Think, new, design** carry meaning. *What do you→Whadja, about→'bout*. |
| **3** | **Declining Politely** | "I can not make it to the meeting." | "**I can't make it** t' the **meeting**." | **Can't** (negative) & **make, meeting** stressed. *Can not* → **can't** (vowel change!). *To* reduced. |
| **4** | **Giving an Opinion** | "It is important that we finish this today." | "**It's im-POR-tant** we **finish this to-DAY**." | Stress on **im-POR-tant** (suffix rule), **finish, today**. *It is→It's, that* dropped. |
| **5** | **Asking for Help** | "Could you please help me with this?" | "**Couldja please help me** with **this**?" | **Help, this** stressed. *Could you→Couldja*. *Please, me, with* rushed/low pitch. |

### Mini-Dialogue: The "Monday Morning Sync"
**A:** "**Hey, didja** see the **up-date** from the **client**?"
**B:** "**Not yet**. **Been** in **meet-ings** all **morn-ing**." *(Stress: Not, yet, Been, meet-, morn-)*
**A:** "**Ba-sic-al-ly**, they **want** the **pro-pos-al** by **Fri-day**."
**B:** "**No prob-lem**. **I'll** get **start-ed** right **after lunch**." *(Stress: Ba-sic-al-ly, want, pro-pos-al, Fri-day, No prob-lem, start-ed, lunch)*

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill 1: The "Beat Tap" Exercise (60 Seconds)
**Instructions:** Read the paragraph below **out loud**. Tap your finger on the table **only on the bolded stressed syllables**. Keep the tempo steady between taps. Crush the unbolded words between the beats.
> "**MAN-age-ment** **WANTS** the **FI-nal** **DRAFT** by **THURS-day**."
> "**PLEASE** **RE-view** the **NUM-bers** and **SEND** me **FEED-back**."

**Goal:** 3 distinct beats per sentence. No pausing. Record yourself.

### Drill 2: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt
**Topic:** *"Describe your current workflow or morning routine."*
**Constraint:** Consciously apply **3 specific reductions** (`gonna`, `wanna`, `hafta`, `d'ya`, `couldja`) and ensure **Content Words pop** (Volume/Length/Pitch). Record for 60 seconds. Listen back: Do the small words disappear?

### Drill 3: Workplace Scenarios

#### Scenario A: The "Quick Pivot" (Stand-up Meeting)
*   **Task:** Explain a sudden priority change to your team in 20 seconds.
*   **Scenario:** The client hated the blue layout. You need the team to switch to green *today*.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**Lis-ten up, ev-ry-one**. **CLI-ent** **RE-ject-ed** the **BLUE** lay-out. **We're SWITCH-ing** to **GREEN** **TO-DAY**. **I NEED** the **AS-sets** by **TWO PM**. **NO ex-cus-es**."

#### Scenario B: The "Polite Pushback" (Email/Slack Follow-up Call)
*   **Task:** Tell a stakeholder their deadline is unrealistic without sounding negative.
*   **Scenario:** They want a full report by EOD. You need 48 hours.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**Hon-est-ly**, **THAT time-line** is **TIGHT**. **I CAN** get you a **SOL-id draft** by **THURS-day morn-ing**. **'LL THAT work** for **YOU**?"

## 5. Daily Implementation Action
**The "Podcast Shadowing" Technique:**
1.  Find a **native speaker podcast** (interview style, not audiobook) — 2 mins max.
2.  Listen to **one sentence**. Pause.
3.  **Whisper** the sentence first, physically **tapping the stressed words** on your leg.
4.  Say it **aloud at full volume** matching the rhythm exactly.
5.  Repeat for 5 minutes during a commute, walk, or dishwashing.

**Success Metric:** You feel the "Morse code" rhythm in your body, not just your mouth.
