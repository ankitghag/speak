# Lesson 2.1: Present Simple — Habits, Facts & General Truths

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 2.1
- **Core Concept:** Present Simple for Routines, Permanent States, and General Facts
- **Learning Objective:** Automate the use of Present Simple to describe *what is generally true*, *what happens regularly*, and *permanent situations* without pausing to translate or calculate grammar.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### The "Default Tense" Mindset
In spoken English, **Present Simple is your default setting.** Use it whenever you are *not* talking about right now (Present Continuous) or a finished past time (Past Simple).

**Three Core Triggers for Spontaneous Speech:**
1.  **Habits & Routines** (Frequency: always, usually, often, sometimes, rarely, never).
2.  **Permanent States / Facts** (Where you live, what you do, languages you speak).
3.  **Schedules & Timetables** (Trains, meetings, opening hours).

### High-Frequency Sentence Formulas

| Formula | Function | Spoken Contraction Focus |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I/You/We/They + Verb (base)** | General facts, habits (Plural/1st/2nd Person) | *I work, We live, They know* |
| **He/She/It + Verb + **s/es** | **Critical:** 3rd Person Singular marker | *She **works**, It **starts**, He **watches*** |
| **Do + I/you/we/they + Verb?** | Yes/No Questions | *Do you need…? / Do they know…?* |
| **Does + he/she/it + Verb (base)?** | Yes/No Questions (Drop the 's' on verb!) | *Does she **like**…? (Not "likes")* |
| **Wh- + do/does + Subject + Verb?** | Open Questions | *What time **does** it **start**? Where **do** you **park**?* |
| **Subject + don't/doesn't + Verb** | Negatives | *I **don't** know. He **doesn't** drive.* |

### The "3rd Person 'S' – Non-Negotiable Clarity Rule"
**In writing, missing the 's' is a typo. In speech, it sounds like a different word or a mistake.**
*   *He work here* → Sounds like "He **walked** here" (Past) or just broken English.
*   *She go* → Sounds like "She **goes**" (missing the 'z' sound).
*   **Drill the vibration:** The 's' adds a syllable only after *s, sh, ch, x, z, o* sounds (**watch-es, go-es, fix-es**). Otherwise, it’s just a buzz: **work**s (z), **stop**s (s), **play**s (z).

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

| Context | ❌ How Not to Say It (Translation/Grammar Focus) | ✅ How a Clear Speaker Says It (Chunked/Flow) | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Describing Role** | "I am working as a manager." | "**I manage** the APAC team." | Present Simple = Permanent role. "Am working" = temporary project. |
| **3rd Person Fact** | "He is knowing the answer." | "**He knows** the answer." | **Stative verbs (know, like, need, understand) NEVER take -ing.** |
| **Negative Habit** | "I not go there often." | "**I don't go** there much." | **Auxiliary 'do' required for negatives.** "Not" cannot sit alone. |
| **Question (3rd Person)** | "Does he *likes* spicy food?" | "**Does he like** spicy food?" | **Signal 'Does' takes the 's'; main verb stays base form.** |
| **Schedule/Timetable** | "The meeting is starting at 9." | "**The meeting starts** at 9 sharp." | Fixed schedules = Present Simple. "Is starting" implies a decision right now. |

### 2-Person Dialogue Snippets

**Snippet 1: Onboarding a New Hire (Describing Routines)**
> **Alex:** "So, **how does the reporting process work** here?"
> **Sam:** "Simple. **We pull** data every Monday. **The dashboard updates** automatically by noon. **I usually review** it Tuesday morning. **Does that make sense**?"

**Snippet 2: Making Plans (Schedules vs. Habits)**
> **Jordan:** "What time **does the last train leave**?"
> **Casey:** "It **leaves** at 11:30. But **it runs** less often on Sundays, so **check the app**."

**Snippet 3: Explaining a Preference (Stative Verbs)**
> **Taylor:** "You **want** the window seat?"
> **Riley:** "Yeah, **I prefer** the view. **I hate** the aisle—people **bump** my elbow."

**Snippet 4: Clarifying a Misunderstanding (Negative + Frequency)**
> **Boss:** "You **don't send** the weekly email?"
> **Employee:** "I **do send** it. **I just don't send** it on Fridays. **I send** it Monday morning."

**Snippet 5: General Truth / Industry Fact**
> **Client:** "**Does this software support** API integration?"
> **Rep:** "Yes, it **handles** REST and GraphQL. **It doesn't require** a separate license."

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill 1: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt (Record Yourself)
**Topic:** *"My Typical Workday (or Weekend)"*
**Constraints:**
1.  Use **only Present Simple**.
2.  Include **at least 3 different frequency adverbs** (usually, rarely, always, sometimes).
3.  Include **one negative** (don't/doesn't).
4.  Include **one 3rd person 's' reference** (a colleague, partner, pet, or schedule).
**Target Time:** 60 seconds. *Listen back: Did the 3rd person 's' sounds clear? Did you avoid "I am doing"?*

---

### Drill 2: Workplace/Real-Life Scenarios

#### Scenario A: Explaining a Process to a Stakeholder
*   **Task:** A client asks how your team handles support tickets.
*   **Scenario:** You need to sound structured and reliable. Use sequencing (First, Then, Finally) + Present Simple.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**First, the system logs** the ticket automatically. **Then, it assigns** it based on region. **A senior engineer reviews** it within an hour. **We don't leave** tickets open overnight. **The client gets** an update at every stage."

#### Scenario B: Negotiating Availability / Setting Boundaries
*   **Task:** A colleague asks you to join a recurring meeting that conflicts with your deep-work block.
*   **Scenario:** State your routine clearly (Fact/Habit) without over-apologizing.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**I block** out Tuesday mornings for deep work. **I don't take** meetings then. **The team knows** this schedule. **Can we move it** to Thursday? **That works** better for my workflow."

## 5. Daily Implementation Action

**The "Narrate the Room" Drill (5 Minutes Today)**
Walk through your home or office (or sit at your desk). **Narrate 10 things you see/hear/know as General Facts or Habits.**
*   *Focus entirely on the **3rd Person 'S' vibration**.*
*   **Examples:**
    *   "The printer **sits** by the window."
    *   "My chair **squeaks** when I lean back."
    *   "Sarah **drinks** coffee at 10 AM."
    *   "This report **needs** a signature."
    *   "The AC **hums** quietly."
*   **Success Criteria:** You physically feel the buzz (z) or hiss (s) at the end of every 3rd person verb. No "He sit," "It need," "She drink."
