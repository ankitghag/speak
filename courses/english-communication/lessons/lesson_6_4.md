# Lesson 6.4: Connected Speech – Linking Words Naturally ("going to" → "gonna", "want to" → "wanna")

### 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 6.4
- **Core Concept:** Reduction & Linking in High-Frequency Phrases
- **Learning Objective:** Automate the natural pronunciation of "going to" → /ˈɡʌnə/ ("gonna") and "want to" → /ˈwɑnə/ ("wanna") in fluent speech, recognizing when to use them for flow vs. when to articulate fully for emphasis.

---

### 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

#### The Mechanics of Reduction
In rapid, natural English, function words and auxiliary verbs lose their distinct vowel sounds and blend into the content words around them. This isn't "lazy" speech—it is the **rhythm engine** of English. Native speakers prioritize *stress-timing* (emphasizing nouns, verbs, adjectives) over *syllable-timing* (pronouncing every vowel clearly).

#### Pattern A: "Going to" → **"Gonna"** /ˈɡʌnə/
*   **Rule:** Occurs *only* when "going to" functions as a future marker (semi-modal) followed by a **base verb**.
*   **Do NOT reduce** when "going" is the main verb indicating physical movement (e.g., "I'm going to the store").
*   **Pronunciation Tip:** The 't' becomes a soft **Flap T** (sounds like a quick 'd'), and the 'ing' drops the 'g'. Stress falls on **GUN**-na.

#### Pattern B: "Want to" → **"Wanna"** /ˈwɑnə/
*   **Rule:** Occurs when "want" is followed directly by an **infinitive verb** (to + verb).
*   **Do NOT reduce** if a noun/pronoun comes between (e.g., "I want *you* to go" → **Never** "I wanna you go").
*   **Pronunciation Tip:** The 't' disappears completely. The vowel in "to" reduces to a schwa /ə/. Stress falls on **WAN**-na.

#### High-Frequency Sentence Formulas
| Structure | Full Form (Slow/Formal) | Reduced Form (Natural Flow) | When to Use Reduced |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Subject + Gonna + Verb** | "I am going to call you." | "**I'm gonna call ya.**" | Default for 90% of future intent statements. |
| **Subject + Wanna + Verb** | "I want to leave early." | "**I wanna leave early.**" | Default for desires/preferences. |
| **Question: Gonna** | "Are you going to stay?" | "**You gonna stay?**" | Casual check-ins; dropping "Are you" is standard. |
| **Question: Wanna** | "Do you want to join us?" | "**Wanna join us?**" | Very common invitation opener; "Do you" often dropped. |
| **Negative: Gonna** | "He is not going to like it." | "**He's not gonna like it.**" | Standard contraction + reduction. |
| **Negative: Wanna** | "I do not want to go." | "**I don't wanna go.**" | Standard contraction + reduction. |

#### When to *Articulate* Fully (Clarity > Flow)
1.  **Emphasis/Contrast:** "I am **GOING TO** do it (whether you like it or not)."
2.  **Formal Authority/Legal/Medical:** "The patient is going to require surgery."
3.  **Non-Native Listener:** If the listener struggles with reductions, switch to clear citation forms.

### 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

#### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

| # | Context | ❌ Robotic / Over-Enunciated (Sounds unnatural, choppy) | ✅ Clear Speaker (Linked, Rhythmic, Professional) | Why the Clear Version Works |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **Team Update** | "I am **go-ing to** send the report by noon." | "**I'm gonna** send the report by noon." | Links Subject+Aux (**I'm**), reduces marker (**gonna**), keeps stress on *send/report/noon*. |
| **2** | **Declining Politely** | "I do **not want to** interrupt the meeting." | "**I don't wanna** interrupt the meeting." | Contractions (**don't**) + reduction (**wanna**) signal polite casualness, not rudeness. |
| **3** | **Quick Question** | "**Are you going to** review the code today?" | "**You gonna** review the code today?" | Drops "Are you" (standard ellipsis), reduces to **gonna**, stress on *review/code*. |
| **4** | **Expressing Preference** | "I **want to** try the new Italian place." | "**I wanna** try the new Italian place." | **Wanna** flows into verb *try*. "Want to" creates a micro-pause that kills momentum. |
| **5** | **Clarifying Movement (NO Reduction)** | "I'm **gonna** the client's office." | "**I'm going to** the client's office." | **Critical Distinction:** "Going" = Main Verb (Movement). Must say "Going TO" (preposition). |

#### 2-Person Dialogue Snippet: Project Planning
> **Alex:** "Hey, **wanna** hop on a quick sync at 2?"
> **Jordan:** "**Gonna** be tough. I **gotta** finish this deck first." *(Note: "gotta" = got to)*
> **Alex:** "Okay. **You gonna** be free by 3?"
> **Jordan:** "Yeah, I **wanna** wrap this up by then. I'll ping you."
> **Alex:** "Sounds good. I'm **gonna** prep the agenda meanwhile."

### 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

#### Drill 1: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt (Record Yourself)
**Topic:** *"My Top 3 Priorities for Tomorrow"*
**Constraint:** You **must** use **"gonna"** for future plans and **"wanna"** for desires at least 3 times each. Do not write a script. Speak extemporaneously.
**Self-Check on Playback:**
1.  Did I say "going to" anywhere I should have said "gonna"?
2.  Did I accidentally say "wanna" before a noun? (e.g., "I wanna coffee" → **Error**. Correct: "I want coffee" or "I wanna *get* coffee").
3.  Is my stress on the *Main Verb* (e.g., **GONNA FINISH**, not *gonna fin-ish*)?

#### Drill 2: Workplace Scenario 1 – The "Soft" Deadline Push
*   **Task:** Nudge a teammate for a deliverable without sounding aggressive.
*   **Scenario:** You need the data from Sam by EOD. You are peers.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "Hey Sam, just checking—**you gonna** have a chance to send that data over today? I **wanna** get the model running before I log off. No sweat if not, just let me know."
*   **Focus:** Dropping "Are you" (**You gonna**), reducing desire (**wanna get**), softeners ("just checking," "no sweat").

#### Drill 3: Workplace Scenario 2 – Stating Intent in a Meeting
*   **Task:** Claim ownership of an action item clearly.
*   **Scenario:** The lead asks, "Who's handling the client follow-up email?"
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**I'm gonna** take that one. I **wanna** make sure we reference the specific pricing discussion we had yesterday."
*   **Focus:** Contraction + Reduction (**I'm gonna**), strong stress on **take**, reduction on desire (**wanna make sure**).

### 5. Daily Implementation Action
**"The 'Gonna/Wanna' Audit"**
During your next **two real-time verbal interactions** (meeting, call, coffee chat, talking to yourself while working):
1.  **Catch yourself** every time you think "I am going to" or "I want to."
2.  **Force the reduction** aloud: **"/ˈɡʌnə/ /ˈwɑnə/"**.
3.  **Immediately check:** Was a verb following? (If noun followed, revert to "want [noun]").
4.  **Goal:** Make the reduced form the *default motor pattern* so your brain stops retrieving the full forms for these specific grammar structures.
