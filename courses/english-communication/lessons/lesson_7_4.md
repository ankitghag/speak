## Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 7.4
- **Core Concept:** Conducting Demos & Product Walkthroughs
- **Learning Objective:** Structure a live demonstration with clear verbal signposting, translate features into user benefits using "So that..." logic, and handle real-time friction (bugs, questions, tangents) without losing narrative momentum.

---

## Theory & Spoken Patterns

### 1. The "Context → Action → Result" (CAR) Narrative Loop
Don't just click buttons; narrate the *why* before the *what*. Users follow intent, not mouse movements.
*   **Formula:** **"Right now I’m going to [ACTION], so you can see [RESULT/BENEFIT]."**
*   **Why:** Pure feature listing ("I click here, then here") creates cognitive load. CAR frames every click as a solution step.

### 2. Explicit Verbal Signposting (The "You are here" markers)
In a screen-share, the audience loses orientation instantly. You are their GPS.
*   **Formulas:**
    *   **Entering:** *"Let’s shift gears and look at the **Reporting Dashboard**."*
    *   **Orienting:** *"You’re looking at the **default view**—notice the date range top-right."*
    *   **Transitioning:** *"We’ve covered setup. **Next up**, I’ll show you the automation engine."*
    *   **Recapping:** *"So, to quickly recap that workflow: **Trigger → Condition → Action**."*

### 3. The "So That" Bridge (Feature → Value Translation)
Never leave a feature hanging. Immediately attach the business outcome.
*   **Formula:** **"This [FEATURE] automates [TASK], **so that** your team can [HIGHER-VALUE OUTCOME]."**
*   **Example:** *"This auto-tagging saves manual sorting, **so that** your support team hits SLA targets without hiring."*

### 4. Managing Live Friction (The "Graceful Recovery" Toolkit)
Demos break. Questions derail. Silence kills credibility. Use these scripts to stay authoritative.

| Situation | Spoken Pattern |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Loading/Buffering** | *"While that spins up, a quick note on the architecture behind this..."* (Fill dead air with context). |
| **Minor Bug/Error** | *"Ah, there’s a known UI quirk in this build—**it doesn’t affect the data**. Let me refresh."* (Own it, minimize it, move on). |
| **Deep-Dive Question (Off-topic)** | *"Great question. **I’ll park that for the Q&A at the end** so we stay on track for the workflow. Remind me if I forget."* |
| **Audience Confusion** | *"Let me pause there—**I want to make sure that landed**. In short: [1-sentence summary]."* |

## Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### 1. Opening the Demo (Setting the Agenda)
*   **How Not to Say It:** *"Okay, I’m sharing my screen. Can you see it? Good. So this is the homepage."*
*   **Clear Speaker:** *"Thanks everyone. **What you’re about to see is the end-to-end onboarding flow**—from invite sent to first login. **Three sections**: Invite Config, User Portal, Admin Verification. **Total time: 10 minutes.** Let’s jump in."*
*   **Why:** Sets expectations (scope, structure, time) so the audience relaxes and follows.

### 2. Narrating a Complex Action (The CAR Loop)
*   **How Not to Say It:** *"I click settings... then integrations... then add new... paste the key... save."*
*   **Clear Speaker:** *"**We need to connect the CRM.** I’m navigating to **Settings > Integrations**—**here’s the key point**: we use a pre-built connector, **so you don’t need dev resources**. I’ll paste the API key... and **hit Save**. **Watch the status badge turn green**—that confirms the handshake."*
*   **Why:** Highlights the *value* (no dev resources) and tells them *what to watch for* (green badge).

### 3. Handling a "Happy Path" vs. "Edge Case" Toggle
*   **How Not to Say It:** *"If it works it looks like this. If it breaks... uh... you get an error."*
*   **Clear Speaker:** *"**This is the happy path**—smooth, under 30 seconds. **But let’s flip the switch** to show the validation layer. I’ll enter an invalid email... **see the inline error?** That’s real-time regex validation **so the user fixes it before submitting**."*
*   **Why:** Explicitly labels the scenario ("Happy Path," "Validation Layer") and explains the UX logic.

### 4. Fielding a Technical Question Mid-Demo
*   **How Not to Say It:** *"Uh, yeah, it scales. Next slide."*
*   **Clear Speaker:** *"**Sharp catch.** The short answer: **yes, it scales horizontally via Kubernetes pods.** **Long answer:** I’ve got a slide in the appendix with load-test numbers. **Let’s capture that for the deep-dive follow-up** so we keep the flow moving. Fair?"*
*   **Why:** Validates the asker ("Sharp catch"), gives the headline answer, defers the deep dive with a concrete place ("appendix slide"), and seeks agreement ("Fair?").

### 5. Closing the Demo (The "So What?" Summary)
*   **How Not to Say It:* *"That’s it. Questions?"*
*   **Clear Speaker:** *"**Let’s land the plane.** We walked through: **Automated Invites** (kills manual admin), **Self-Serve Portal** (cuts support tickets by 40%), **Instant Verification** (compliance ready on Day 1). **Next step:** I’ll send the sandbox link + that scaling doc. **Any blockers before we wrap?**"
*   **Why:** Recaps *benefits* (not features), defines concrete next steps, and invites specific feedback ("blockers").

## Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### 1. One-Minute Speaking Prompt
**Topic:** *"Walk me through the last feature you shipped (or a tool you use daily) as if I’m a non-technical stakeholder. Use the CAR loop for every click. Record yourself."*
*   **Focus:** No jargon without definition. Explicit "So that..." bridges. Verbal signposts ("First," "Now," "Finally").

### 2. Workplace Scenario A: The "Executive Drop-In"
*   **Task:** A VP joins the last 5 minutes of your 30-min demo. You must re-orient them instantly without boring the room.
*   **Scenario:** You are mid-way through the "Automation Builder." VP asks: *"Where are we?"*
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > *"**Welcome, [Name].** We’re **15 minutes in, halfway through the Automation Builder**—specifically showing how **Marketing can build a lead-nurture sequence without engineering**. **Last step:** we set the trigger. **Next:** we define the email cadence. **Happy to dive deeper post-call on the ROI numbers.**"*

### 3. Workplace Scenario B: The "Screen Freeze" Recovery
*   **Task:** Your screen share freezes for 10 seconds. You regain control. You must recover authority and context immediately.
*   **Scenario:** You were demonstrating a "Drag-and-Drop Dashboard Builder."
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > *"**Tech gremlins—thanks for the patience.** **We’re back.** Just to re-orient: **you’re looking at the blank canvas**. **Before the freeze, I’d added the 'Revenue' widget.** **Now I’m dragging 'Churn Rate' next to it** so leadership sees the full picture side-by-side. **Watch how the layout snaps to grid...** there it is."*

## Daily Implementation Action
**Action:** **Record a 2-minute "Micro-Demo" of a mundane task** (e.g., "How I filter my inbox," "Setting a Slack reminder," "My coffee routine").
1.  **Script it first** using: **Context → Action → Result** for every step.
2.  **Record it** (phone voice memo or Loom).
3.  **Listen back** specifically for: **Dead air** (silence > 2 sec), **Missing "So that" bridges**, **Assumed context** (did you say *where* you are in the UI?).
4.  **Re-record once** fixing the top 2 flaws.
**Goal:** Build the "narrator muscle" so live demos feel like second nature.
