# Lesson 5.3: Common Spoken Collocations (Natural Word Pairs)

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 5.3
- **Core Concept:** High-frequency verb-noun and adjective-noun pairings that native speakers use automatically to sound fluent and precise.
- **Learning Objective:** Replace generic, "safe" verbs (do, make, get, have, very) with precise, natural collocations to increase clarity, professionalism, and speaking speed in workplace and daily conversations.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### Why Collocations Matter for Speech
Grammar rules allow "make a decision" or "do a decision," but **only "make a decision" sounds natural.** Using the wrong pair ("do a decision," "strong rain," "fast food" vs "quick meal") creates micro-hesitations in the listener's brain. Mastering the top 50–100 spoken collocations eliminates "translation lag" and makes you sound decisive.

### The "De-Verb" Strategy (Replacing Generic Verbs)
Stop defaulting to **Get, Have, Do, Make, Give, Take**. Swap them for the specific verb that "lives" with that noun.

| Generic Verb + Noun (Avoid) | **Natural Collocation (Use This)** | Spoken Context |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Get** an email / a call | **Receive** an email / **Take** a call | Formal/Professional |
| **Get** permission / approval | **Secure** approval / **Obtain** permission | Projects/Negotiations |
| **Have** a meeting / lunch | **Hold** a meeting / **Grab** lunch | Scheduling/Casual |
| **Do** a presentation / report | **Give / Deliver** a presentation / **Compile / Write** a report | Performance/Output |
| **Make** a mistake / decision | **Make** a mistake (Keep) / **Make / Reach** a decision | *Exception: "Make" stays for mistakes/decisions* |
| **Give** advice / feedback | **Offer** advice / **Provide / Share** feedback | Mentoring/Reviews |
| **Take** a look / break | **Have** a look (UK) / **Take** a look (US) / **Take** a break | Standard |

### High-Impact Adjective-Noun Pairs (Intensifiers)
Replace **"Very + Adjective"** with a single strong collocation. This cuts syllable count and adds punch.

| Weak "Very" Phrase | **Strong Collocation** | Why it works |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Very important | **Critical / Crucial / Vital** | Signals urgency instantly |
| Very difficult | **Challenging / Demanding / Tricky** | "Tricky" implies solvable but complex |
| Very busy | **Swamped / Slammed / Packed** | Visceral, visual language |
| Very good/great | **Outstanding / Solid / Impressive** | Specific praise lands better |
| Very bad/serious | **Severe / Critical / Disastrous** | Accurate severity scaling |
| Very sure | **Certain / Confident / Convinced** | Removes doubt from your tone |

### Spoken Formulas for Immediate Use
1.  **"We need to [Strong Verb] the [Noun]."** → *We need to **address** the issue / **bridge** the gap / **meet** the deadline.*
2.  **"That’s a [Strong Adj] [Noun]."** → *That’s a **valid** point / **tight** deadline / **rough** estimate.*
3.  **"Let’s [Verb] a [Noun]."** → *Let’s **run** a test / **schedule** a sync / **float** an idea.*

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

| Situation | ❌ Learner Style (Grammatically OK, Sounds Off) | ✅ Clear Speaker (Natural Collocation) | Coaching Note |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Project Update** | "We **did** good progress today." | "We **made** solid progress today." | *Progress collocates with **make**, not do.* |
| **Problem Solving** | "We need to **find** a solution fast." | "We need to **find / come up with** a workaround." | *Solution = find/develop. Workaround = come up with.* |
| **Scheduling** | "Can we **make** a meeting for 3 PM?" | "Can we **set up / schedule** a sync for 3 PM?" | *Meetings: Set up, schedule, hold. "Make a meeting" sounds childish.* |
| **Giving Opinion** | "I **have** a different opinion." | "I **see it differently** / I **have a different take**." | *"Have an opinion" is static. "Take" or "See it" shows active thinking.* |
| **Workload** | "I am **very busy** this week." | "I’m **swamped / slammed** this week." | *"Very busy" is flat. "Swamped" conveys volume + stress instantly.* |

### Mini-Dialogue: The Project Handoff
**Context:** Two colleagues (Alex & Jamie) handing off a task quickly.

**Alex:** "Hey, did you **get a chance to look at** the brief?"
**Jamie:** "Yeah, I **had a quick scan**. The timeline looks **tight**—honestly, it’s **borderline impossible** without more resources."
**Alex:** "Agreed. I’ll **flag the risk** to the lead. Can you **put together** a rough estimate for the extra headcount?"
**Jamie:** "Sure. I’ll **draft something up** by lunch."
**Alex:** "Perfect. **Appreciate the quick turnaround**."

**Collocation Breakdown:**
*   *Get a chance to look at* (Standard polite request)
*   *Have a scan / glance / look* (Noun + Light Verb)
*   *Tight timeline / Borderline impossible* (Adjective Intensifiers)
*   *Flag the risk / Raise a concern* (Professional reporting)
*   *Put together / Draft up / Pull together* (Creating documents)
*   *Quick turnaround* (Standard business praise)

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill 1: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt
**Topic:** *"Describe a recent work challenge using ZERO generic verbs (no get, have, do, make, give, take) and ZERO 'very + adjective' combinations."*
*   **Instructions:** Record yourself for 60 seconds. Focus entirely on verb-noun precision.
*   **Target Collocations to force in:** *Resolve an issue, meet a deadline, secure approval, compile data, tight bandwidth, critical path, rough draft.*

### Drill 2: Workplace Scenarios

#### Scenario A: The "Scope Creep" Pushback
*   **Task:** A stakeholder asks for a new feature mid-sprint. You must say no professionally but collaboratively.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "Thanks for flagging that. Adding scope **poses a risk** to the current **delivery date**. We’re already running at **full capacity**. Let’s **park this** in the backlog and **revisit the priority** during next sprint planning. Does that **work for you**?"

#### Scenario B: The "Monday Morning Sync" (Casual/Status)
*   **Task:** Give a 30-second verbal status update to your manager while walking to coffee.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "Weekend was good, thanks. On the work front: I **wrapped up** the client deck Friday. I’m **waiting on sign-off** from Legal—should **come through** today. Main **focus this week** is **finalizing** the Q3 budget. I’ll **keep you posted** on the Legal bottleneck."

## 5. Daily Implementation Action

**"The Collocation Swap" (Real-Time Practice):**
During **one** meeting, Slack/Teams thread, or email today:
1.  Catch yourself typing/saying a generic verb (*get, have, do, make, give, take*) or *very + adjective*.
2.  **Pause.** Swap it for the precise collocation *before* you hit send or speak.
3.  **Log the swap** in a note on your phone (e.g., *"Swapped 'get approval' → 'secure approval'"*).

**Goal:** 3 conscious swaps today. This builds the "mental dictionary" muscle memory faster than passive study.
