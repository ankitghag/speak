# Lesson 1.2: Subject-Verb Agreement — Fixing Common Plural/Singular Verbal Errors

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 1.2
- **Core Concept:** Subject-Verb Agreement in Real-Time Speech
- **Learning Objective:** Automatically match verbs to their true subjects—especially with tricky nouns, compound subjects, and interrupting phrases—so your spoken English sounds polished and professional without hesitation.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### Why This Trips Up Speakers
In writing, you can backtrack. In speech, the verb flies out before you’ve confirmed the subject. The three most common "agreement traps" in live conversation are:

1. **Distance Traps** – A long phrase separates subject and verb.  
   *The box of files **are** on the desk.* ❌ → *The box of files **is** on the desk.* ✅  
   **Rule:** The verb agrees with the *head noun* (box), not the nearest noun (files).

2. **Collective Nouns** – Team, staff, family, audience, committee.  
   - US default: treat as **singular** (*The team **has** decided*).  
   - UK/commonwealth: often **plural** when emphasizing individuals (*The team **have** gone home*).  
   **Practical tip:** Pick one convention (usually singular in global business) and stay consistent.

3. **Compound Subjects with *or / nor / either…or / neither…nor***  
   Verb agrees with the **closer** subject.  
   *Neither the manager nor the analysts **were** available.* ✅  
   *Neither the analysts nor the manager **was** available.* ✅

### High-Frequency Spoken Formulas
| Pattern | Formula | Example |
|---------|---------|---------|
| **Head-noun anchor** | *The [singular head] of [plural modifier] **is/has**…* | *The list of candidates **is** final.* |
| **Collective as unit** | *The [collective] **has/does/is**…* | *Marketing **has** approved the budget.* |
| **Proximity rule** | *Neither [A] nor [B] **verb-match-B**…* | *Neither the CEO nor the VPs **attend**.* |
| **Indefinite pronouns** | *Everyone/Each/Nobody **has/does/is**…* | *Everyone **needs** a badge.* |

### When & Why to Use Them
- **Meetings & updates:** “The scope of deliverables **has** changed.” (Not *have*)  
- **Status reports:** “Nobody **knows** the exact deadline.” (Not *know*)  
- **Negotiations:** “Either the discount or the extended terms **works** for us.”  
Consistent agreement signals precision—critical for credibility.

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### 5 Realistic Spoken Examples
| How Not to Say It | How a Clear Speaker Says It | Why |
|-------------------|-----------------------------|-----|
| “The team **are** working late.” | “The team **is** working late.” | Treat collective as singular unit (US/global norm). |
| “A box of pens **were** missing.” | “A box of pens **was** missing.” | Head noun = *box* (singular). |
| “Neither the client nor the lawyers **has** replied.” | “Neither the client nor the lawyers **have** replied.” | Verb matches closer subject (*lawyers*). |
| “Each of the proposals **look** good.” | “Each of the proposals **looks** good.” | *Each* is always singular. |
| “There **is** three issues.” | “There **are** three issues.” | *There* follows the real subject (*issues*). |

### 2-Person Dialogue Snippet
**Context:** Project sync—quick, natural, agreement-clean.

> **Alex:** “The latest batch of metrics **looks** solid. Neither the outliers nor the average **has** shifted.”  
> **Sam:** “Good. The QA team **has** signed off, but the dev leads **want** one more regression pass.”  
> **Alex:** “Understood. Everyone **agrees** on the timeline?”  
> **Sam:** “Each of the stakeholders **confirms** Friday. We’re set.”

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### 1-Minute Speaking Prompt
**Topic:** *“Explain a recent project change using at least three different agreement patterns (collective noun, head-noun anchor, proximity rule).”*  
**Instructions:** Record yourself. Listen for any verb that doesn’t match its true subject. Re-record until clean.

### Workplace/Real-Life Scenarios

#### Scenario A — Status Email Read-Aloud
**Task:** Read this update aloud as if briefing your manager.  
**Script:**  
> “The set of requirements **has** been finalized. Neither the vendor nor the internal reviewers **have** flagged risks. The steering committee **meets** Thursday; each member **receives** the deck tomorrow.”

#### Scenario B — Impromptu Hallway Update
**Task:** A colleague asks, “How’s the rollout?” Respond in 15 seconds.  
**Model Response:**  
> “The pilot group **is** stable. A handful of edge cases **remain**, but the support squad **handles** them fast. Everyone **expects** GA next Monday.”

## 5. Daily Implementation Action
**Action:** Pick **one** meeting, call, or voice note today. Before you speak, silently identify the *head noun* of your first sentence. Say the sentence, then mentally score: *Did the verb match the head noun?* Note one correction for tomorrow.
