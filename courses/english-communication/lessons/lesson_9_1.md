# Lesson 9.1: The PREP Framework for Structuring Thoughts (Point → Reason → Example → Point)

---

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 9.1
- **Core Concept:** The PREP Framework (Point, Reason, Example, Point)
- **Learning Objective:** By the end of this lesson, you will be able to structure spontaneous spoken responses clearly and persuasively using the PREP method, eliminating rambling and ensuring your key message lands effectively in meetings, interviews, and daily conversations.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### What is PREP?
PREP is a **mental scaffold** for speaking "off the cuff." It forces logical flow: **Claim → Justification → Proof → Reinforcement.** It works because listeners process information best when the *conclusion* comes first (Bottom Line Up Front), followed by the supporting logic.

### The 4 Steps (Mental Checklist)
1.  **POINT (The "Headline"):** State your answer, opinion, or decision **in one sentence**. No fluff.
2.  **REASON (The "Why"):** Explain the logic or cause. Use *because, since, driven by, based on...*
3.  **EXAMPLE (The "Proof"):** Give a specific, concrete instance. Use *for instance, last week, take Project X, a quick story...*
4.  **POINT (The "Anchor"):** Restate the main message differently to close. Use *So, bottom line..., That’s why..., To wrap up...*

### High-Frequency Spoken Formulas

| Stage | Formula Starters (Say these naturally) |
| :--- | :--- |
| **POINT** | "The short answer is..." / "My take is..." / "I recommend we..." / "Bottom line:..." |
| **REASON** | "This is mainly because..." / "The driver here is..." / "Since we need to [goal]..." |
| **EXAMPLE** | "For example, last quarter..." / "Take yesterday’s meeting..." / "A concrete case:..." |
| **POINT (Close)** | "So, circling back..." / "That’s why I’m saying..." / "Ultimately, it comes down to..." |

### When to Use It (Real Speech Contexts)
- **Q&A sessions:** "What do you think about the budget cut?"
- **Status Updates:** "How is the project going?"
- **Persuasion/Pitching:** "Why should we choose Vendor A?"
- **Conflict Resolution:** "Why are you pushing back on this deadline?"
- **Networking/Small Talk:** "What’s your take on the new AI tools?"

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### A. "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

#### Scenario 1: Boss asks, "Should we hire an intern this summer?"
| ❌ **Rambling (No Structure)** | ✅ **PREP Structure** |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Um, well, it depends. I mean, we have budget, right? But also the team is swamped. Like, last week Sarah had to stay till 8 PM. But then again, training takes time. So maybe? I don't know, what do you think?" | **POINT:** "Yes, we should hire one. <br>**REASON:** The team is at capacity and burnout risk is high. <br>**EXAMPLE:** Last week Sarah pulled an 8 PM shift just to clear the ticket backlog. <br>**POINT:** **An intern solves the bandwidth gap without the cost of a senior hire.**" |
| **Why it fails:** Listener doesn't know your stance until the end (or ever). | **Why it works:** Decision first. Logic second. Proof third. Decision reinforced last. |

#### Scenario 2: Client asks, "Why is the timeline 6 weeks instead of 4?"
| ❌ **Defensive/Disjointed** | ✅ **PREP Structure** |
| :--- | :--- |
| "Four weeks is impossible. There's too much risk. The API integration is tricky. Remember Project Delta? That failed. So we need buffer. Six weeks is safe." | **POINT:** "Six weeks is the realistic minimum for a stable launch. <br>**REASON:** The API integration carries high technical debt risk. <br>**EXAMPLE:** On Project Delta, rushing that exact integration caused a two-week rollback. <br>**POINT:** **Six weeks protects your launch date from slipping further.**" |
| **Why it fails:** Sounds like excuses; "No" feels abrupt. | **Why it works:** Frames the timeline as *protection* for the client, not an obstacle. |

### B. 3 Additional Realistic Spoken Examples

**1. Meeting: "What’s the status on the marketing revamp?"**
> **POINT:** "We’re on track for the Friday demo. **REASON:** The design system audit finished ahead of schedule. **EXAMPLE:** The team finalized the component library Tuesday—two days early. **POINT:** **So we’re green for Friday; no blockers right now.**"

**2. Interview: "Tell me about a time you handled a difficult stakeholder."**
> **POINT:** "I align them by translating tech constraints into business risk. **REASON:** Stakeholders usually push back because they don’t see the trade-offs. **EXAMPLE:** Last year, Sales wanted a feature in Sprint 5. I showed them the revenue impact of delaying the security patch instead. They agreed to Sprint 8. **POINT:** **That’s how I turn 'No' into 'Not now, here’s why.'**"

**3. Casual/Networking: "Do you like the new hybrid policy?"**
> **POINT:** "Honestly, it’s a net positive for me. **REASON:** The fixed anchor days (Tue/Thu) solve the 'empty office' problem. **EXAMPLE:** Last month, I ran into the VP of Product at the coffee machine on a Tuesday—we unblocked a spec in 10 mins that would’ve taken 3 emails. **POINT:** **Those collisions are worth the commute.**"

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill 1: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt (Record Yourself)
**Topic:** *"Should our company mandate a 'No-Meeting Friday' policy?"*
**Instructions:** Set a timer for 60 seconds. Stand up. Answer **only using PREP**.
1.  **Point:** Yes/No/Modified.
2.  **Reason:** Productivity / Collaboration / Autonomy.
3.  **Example:** Specific anecdote (yours or hypothetical).
4.  **Point:** Final summary sentence.
*Listen back: Did you hit all 4? Was the final Point distinct from the first?*

---

### Drill 2: Workplace Scenario Roleplay

#### Scenario A: The "Scope Creep" Pushback
*   **Task:** A stakeholder asks you to add a "small feature" to the current sprint which is already full.
*   **Scenario:** You are the Tech Lead. You must say "No" (or "Not now") clearly without sounding unhelpful.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**POINT:** We can’t add it to *this* sprint without cutting something else. **REASON:** The team’s capacity is locked at 100% for the compliance release. **EXAMPLE:** If we drop the encryption module to fit this in, we fail the audit next Friday. **POINT:** **Let’s park this in the backlog for Sprint 12—I’ll groom it with the PO tomorrow.**"

#### Scenario B: The "Impromptu Update" (Skip-Level Meeting)
*   **Task:** Your Director’s Director stops you in the hall: "How’s the migration project looking?"
*   **Scenario:** You have 30 seconds before they walk into their next meeting. No slides.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "**POINT:** Migration is 80% done and low risk. **REASON:** We mitigated the data latency issue last sprint. **EXAMPLE:** Stress tests Monday showed 40ms response times—well under our 100ms SLA. **POINT:** **We’re targeting code freeze next Wednesday; on track for June 1st launch.**"

## 5. Daily Implementation Action

**🎯 The "3-PREP Challenge"**
Today, **force yourself to use PREP exactly 3 times** in live conversation (Slack/Teams voice notes count; text/email does not—this is a *speech* drill).

**Target Moments:**
1.  **Status Update:** (Stand-up, 1:1, or async voice note).
2.  **Opinion/Decision:** (Code review comment, design discussion, lunch chat).
3.  **Answering a "Why":** (Any question starting with "Why did we...", "Should we...", "What about...").

**Accountability Hack:** Put a sticky note on your monitor with **"P-R-E-P"**. Check off a letter each time you consciously hit a stage. Aim for 3 full cycles before 5 PM.
