# Lesson 9.4: Impromptu Speaking – Talking On-the-Spot for 1–2 Minutes

---

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 9.4
- **Core Concept:** Structuring spontaneous speech using the **PREP** framework (Point, Reason, Example, Point) to speak coherently for 1–2 minutes without preparation.
- **Learning Objective:** By the end of this lesson, you will be able to organize your thoughts instantly under pressure, eliminate rambling, and deliver a clear, persuasive mini-speech in meetings, interviews, or social settings.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### The PREP Framework (Your Mental Scaffold)
When put on the spot, your brain freezes because it’s trying to *find* content *and* *structure* simultaneously. PREP separates them. You only need to find **one main idea**; the structure handles the rest.

| Step | Label | Function | Time Target | Mental Prompt |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1** | **P – Point** | State your main answer/opinion immediately. | 10–15 sec | *"My take is..."* / *"The short answer is..."* |
| **2** | **R – Reason** | Explain *why* you hold this view. | 20–30 sec | *"The reason is..."* / *"This matters because..."* |
| **3** | **E – Example** | Give *one* concrete story, data point, or anecdote. | 30–60 sec | *"For instance..."* / *"Let me give you a quick example..."* |
| **4** | **P – Point** | Summarize and close strongly. | 10–15 sec | *"So, bottom line..."* / *"That’s why I believe..."* |

### High-Frequency "Buying Time" Formulas
*Use these in the first 3 seconds while your brain grabs the Point.*

1.  **"That’s a great question. My immediate thought is…"** (Buys 2–3 sec, sounds confident).
2.  **"Let me put it this way…"** (Signals you are structuring the answer).
3.  **"If I had to summarize it in one sentence: …"** (Forces you into the Point step instantly).

### When & Why to Use This
- **Workplace Q&A:** "What’s your take on the new policy?" → PREP prevents rambling.
- **Job Interviews:** "Tell me about a time you failed." → Point = The Lesson; Example = The Story.
- **Social/Networking:** "What do you think about [current event]?" → Keeps you conversational but substantive.

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

#### Scenario A: Sudden Meeting Question ("What’s the risk on Project X?")
| Style | Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| ❌ **Rambling** | "Uh, well, there’s a few things. The timeline is tight, obviously. And the budget... we might run over. Also the vendor hasn't confirmed. So yeah, it's risky." |
| ✅ **PREP Structured** | "**Point:** The single biggest risk is **vendor dependency**. **Reason:** We have zero slack in the timeline if they deliver late. **Example:** Last quarter, Vendor B was two weeks late on the API, and it pushed our launch back a month. **Point:** So unless we get a written commitment by Friday, we should activate the backup vendor." |

#### Scenario B: Interview ("Why do you want this role?")
| Style | Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| ❌ **Generic/Unstructured** | "I like your company. It seems cool. I need a job where I can grow. I saw the job description and it matches my skills. I'm a hard worker." |
| ✅ **PREP Structured** | "**Point:** I want this role because **it sits at the intersection of data and customer strategy**, which is exactly where I add the most value. **Reason:** In my last role, I realized I’m not just an analyst—I drive retention strategies. **Example:** I built a churn model that flagged at-risk accounts *before* they left, saving $200k ARR in six months. **Point:** I want to bring that same proactive approach to your Customer Success team." |

#### Scenario C: Casual Debate ("Is remote work better?")
| Style | Speech |
| :--- | :--- |
| ❌ **Waffling** | "It depends. Some people like office. Some like home. I guess hybrid is best? But commuting sucks. Yeah, hybrid." |
| ✅ **PREP Structured** | "**Point:** **Hybrid is the only sustainable model** for knowledge work. **Reason:** Pure remote kills spontaneous innovation; pure office kills deep focus. **Example:** My team tried 'Core Days' (Tues-Thurs in office). Meeting load dropped 30%, but our sprint velocity stayed flat. **Point:** So, mandatory office days for collaboration, optional for execution—that’s the sweet spot." |

### Quick Dialogue Snippet: The "Hot Seat"
**Context:** Senior Lead asks for an update on a blockers *right now* in a stand-up.

**Lead:** *"Alex, the client asked for the compliance report yesterday. Where do we stand?"*

**Alex (Internal Monologue: *Point: Delayed. Reason: Legal review. Example: Specific clause. Point: ETA Friday.)***

**Alex:** **"The short answer: **It’s delayed 48 hours.** **Reason:** Legal flagged the data privacy clause in Section 4. **Example:** They need the new SOC-2 addendum attached before they sign off—we sent that request to the vendor this morning. **Point:** I’ll have the final PDF to you by **Friday 10 AM**."**

**Lead:** *"Good. Flag me if the vendor doesn't reply by noon."*

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill 1: The 60-Second PREP Recording
**Instructions:** Set a timer for **60 seconds**. Pick **ONE** prompt below. Record yourself (phone voice memo). Use PREP explicitly. Do *not* stop recording until the timer hits 60s. Listen back: Did you hit all 4 steps?

**Prompts (Pick One):**
1.  "Should meetings have a 'no laptop' policy?"
2.  "What is the one skill every junior hire is missing?"
3.  "Convince me to try your favorite lunch spot."
4.  "Why is [Your Current Project] important to the company?"

**Self-Check:**
- [ ] **Point:** Was my opinion clear in the first 15s?
- [ ] **Reason:** Did I explain *why* without repeating the point?
- [ ] **Example:** Was it specific (names, numbers, a moment in time)?
- [ ] **Point:** Did I land the plane, or just trail off?

---

### Drill 2: Workplace Scenario – "The Hallway Pitch"
**Task:** You run into the VP of Product in the kitchen. She asks: *"Hey, I heard you're testing the new onboarding flow. Worth the effort?"*

**Scenario:** You have data, but only 90 seconds before she walks away.
**Model Spoken Response:**
> "**Point:** **Absolutely—early data shows a 15% lift in Day-7 retention.** **Reason:** The old flow had a 'dead zone' at Step 3 where users dropped off to check email. **Example:** We added a progress bar and a 'skip for now' button. In the A/B test, the variant group completed onboarding 40% faster. **Point:** We’re rolling it to 100% next Monday; I’ll send the deck today."

---

### Drill 3: Social Scenario – "The 'Hot Take' at Dinner"
**Task:** Friend group discusses: *"Is AI going to replace creative jobs?"* You disagree with the doom-mongering.

**Scenario:** You want to sound thoughtful, not argumentative.
**Model Spoken Response:**
> "**Point:** **AI won't replace creatives; it will replace creatives *who don't use AI*.** **Reason:** The value is shifting from *execution* (writing code, drafting copy) to *curation and direction*. **Example:** Last week, I used Midjourney to storyboard a pitch deck in 20 mins. It didn't design the strategy—I did. It just removed the blank-page paralysis. **Point:** So the job becomes 'Creative Director of AI tools,' not 'Pixel Pusher.' That’s a promotion, not a threat."**

## 5. Daily Implementation Action

**🎯 The "One Meeting, One PREP" Rule**
**Action:** In your **very next meeting or conversation** (virtual or in-person) where you are asked a question or give an update, **consciously run the PREP loop once.**

**How to execute:**
1.  Hear the question.
2.  Say: *"The main point is..."* (Point).
3.  Say: *"Because..."* (Reason).
4.  Say: *"For example..."* (Example - keep it to 2 sentences max).
5.  Say: *"So..."* (Point).

**Success Metric:** You feel the "landing" of the final Point. The listener nods or asks a relevant follow-up (not "Could you repeat that?").
