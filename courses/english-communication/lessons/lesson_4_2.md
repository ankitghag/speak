# Lesson 4.2: Intention Patterns – "I'm planning to...", "I would like to...", "I was thinking of..."

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 4.2
- **Core Concept:** Expressing future intentions with varying degrees of certainty and formality.
- **Learning Objective:** By the end of this lesson, you will be able to distinguish between firm plans, polite desires, and tentative ideas, and select the correct pattern to sound natural and precise in workplace and social conversations.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### The "Certainty Scale" of Intentions
Native speakers choose specific phrases based on **how decided** the action is and **who they are talking to**.

| Pattern | Certainty Level | Tone / Context | Grammar Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I'm planning to...** | **High (80–90%)** | Professional, decided, active preparation underway. | `I'm planning to + [base verb]` |
| **I would like to...** | **Medium–High (Desire)** | Polite, professional, often a request or goal (not yet scheduled). | `I would like to + [base verb]` <br>*(Contracted: **I'd like to**)* |
| **I was thinking of...** | **Low–Medium (Tentative)** | Casual, exploratory, "floating an idea," seeking feedback. | `I was thinking of + [verb-ing]` |

---

### High-Frequency Sentence Formulas

#### 1. `I'm planning to...` (The "Locked In" Plan)
*Use when: You have made the decision, maybe bought tickets, booked time, or told stakeholders.*
> **Formula:** `I'm planning to [verb] [time reference / detail].`
> *   "I'm planning to **submit the report by Friday**."
> *   "We're planning to **launch the beta next month**."

#### 2. `I'd like to...` (The Professional Want / Soft Request)
*Use when: Stating a goal in a review, asking permission politely, or networking. **Always contract "would" to "'d" in speech.***
> **Formula:** `I'd like to [verb] [object/reason].`
> *   "I'd like to **schedule a quick sync with you**."
> *   "I'd like to **move into a project lead role eventually**."

#### 3. `I was thinking of...` / `I was thinking about...` (The "Trial Balloon")
*Use when: Brainstorming, suggesting something softly, or admitting a half-formed idea. **Past continuous ("was thinking") creates distance = politeness/tentativeness.***
> **Formula:** `I was thinking of [verb-ing] [optional: what do you think?].`
> *   "I was thinking of **skipping the meeting to focus on the deck**."
> *   "I was thinking about **ordering pizza for the team**—sound good?"

---

### Real Speech Nuance: "Going to" vs. These Patterns
*   **"I'm going to..."** = Neutral prediction or immediate decision ("I'm going to grab coffee *right now*").
*   **"I'm planning to..."** = Process-oriented ("I'm planning to *study for the cert exam over the summer*").
*   **"I'd like to..."** = Preference/Goal ("I'd like to *get certified eventually*").
*   **"I was thinking of..."** = Hypothesis ("I was thinking of *taking that cert, but it's expensive*").

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

| Situation | ❌ How Not to Say It (Unnatural / Wrong Tone) | ✅ How a Clear Speaker Says It | Why? |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Telling your boss about a decided vacation.** | "I would like to take next week off." | "**I'm planning to** take next week off. I've already covered my tasks with Sarah." | "Would like" sounds like a request/hope. "Planning to" signals it's arranged. |
| **2. Suggesting a new tool in a team meeting.** | "I plan to suggest we use Notion." | "**I was thinking of** suggesting Notion for the wiki. Thoughts?" | "Plan to" sounds rigid/solo. "Thinking of" invites collaboration. |
| **3. Answering "Where do you see yourself in 5 years?"** | "I plan to be a Director." | "**I'd like to** move toward a Director track, ideally leading a cross-functional team." | "Plan to" sounds arrogant/rigid for goals. "Would like to" shows ambition + flexibility. |
| **4. Declining an invite because of a prior commitment.** | "I was thinking of going to the gym." | "**I'm planning to** hit the gym at 6, so I can't make drinks." | "Thinking of" implies you might skip it. "Planning to" sets a firm boundary. |
| **5. Asking a colleague for a favor.** | "I plan to ask you to review this." | "**I'd like to** ask you to review this when you have a sec." | "Plan to" announces intent awkwardly. "Would like to" *is* the polite request. |

---

### Mini-Dialogue 1: Project Kick-off (Certainty vs. Desire)
**Alex (PM):** "Okay, for the Q3 rollout, **I'm planning to** freeze the scope by Wednesday. **I'd like to** get design sign-off before then."
**Jordan (Lead):** "Got it. **I was thinking of** running a quick usability test Tuesday—would that help?"
**Alex:** "Perfect. Let's do it."

### Mini-Dialogue 2: Career Chat (Tentative vs. Goal)
**Manager:** "Any thoughts on next steps for you?"
**You:** "**I'd like to** lead a client-facing project this year. **I was thinking of** shadowing Priya on the Acme account to learn the ropes, but **I'm planning to** apply for the internal mentor program regardless."

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill A: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt (Record Yourself)
**Prompt:** *"Walk me through your next 30 days. Use all three patterns at least once."*
*   **Structure your answer:**
    1.  One firm commitment (*I'm planning to...*)
    2.  One professional goal/request (*I'd like to...*)
    3.  One idea you are floating/unsure about (*I was thinking of...*)

**Target Time:** 60 seconds. **Focus:** Smooth contractions (*I'm, I'd, I was*), clear "ing" endings on the third pattern.

---

### Drill B: Workplace Scenarios (Speak the Model Response Out Loud)

#### Scenario 1: The "Scope Creep" Meeting
*   **Task:** A stakeholder asks for a new feature *today*. You cannot do it, but you want to help later.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "We **can't squeeze it in this sprint**—the devs are at capacity. **I'm planning to** lock the backlog by EOD Friday. However, **I'd like to** slot this for the top of the next cycle. **I was thinking of** scheduling a 15-min scoping call next week to define it properly. Does that work?"

#### Scenario 2: Networking Event / Coffee Chat
*   **Task:** Someone asks, "So, what's next for you?" You are exploring a pivot but have a current duty.
*   **Model Spoken Response:**
    > "Right now **I'm planning to** finish the migration project by June. Long term, **I'd like to** transition into Product Management. **I was thinking of** doing the Google PM cert at night, but I'm still weighing the cost vs. value. What's your take?"

## 5. Daily Implementation Action

**The "Three-Bucket" Journal / Voice Note:**
Before your day starts (or during your commute), **speak aloud (or record a 30-second voice note)** categorizing your top 3 items for today into the three buckets:

1.  **The Locked In:** "I'm planning to [firm task]." (e.g., *send the invoice, pick up kids*)
2.  **The Ask/Goal:** "I'd like to [goal/request]." (e.g., *get feedback on the deck, leave by 5*)
3.  **The Maybe:** "I was thinking of [tentative idea]." (e.g., *trying that new salad place, refactoring the login code*)

**Success Criteria:** You say all three patterns naturally without reading them off a screen. This trains your brain to *sort* intentions by certainty automatically.
