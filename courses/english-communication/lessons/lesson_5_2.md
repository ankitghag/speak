# Lesson 5.2: Essential Phrasal Verbs — Pick up, Look into, Carry out, Turn out, Point out

## 1. Lesson Information
- **Day/Topic Number:** 5.2
- **Core Concept:** High-utility separable and inseparable phrasal verbs for professional and daily communication.
- **Learning Objective:** Use these five phrasal verbs naturally in spoken English to replace formal, stiff vocabulary (e.g., "investigate" → *look into*, "execute" → *carry out*) for clearer, more confident communication.

---

## 2. Theory & Spoken Patterns

### Why Phrasal Verbs Matter in Speech
Native speakers use phrasal verbs **constantly** in conversation. Using the formal Latinate equivalent ("I will *investigate* this") often sounds robotic, distant, or overly academic in a meeting or casual chat. Mastering these five shifts your register from "textbook" to "professional natural."

### The 5 Target Verbs: Mechanics & Formulas

#### 1. Pick up (Separable)
*Meaning A: Collect a person/thing | Meaning B: Learn casually | Meaning C: Resume/Improve*
- **Pattern:** `Pick [object] up` / `Pick up [object]` (Pronouns *must* go in middle: *Pick **it** up*)
- **Spoken Formula:** *"I’ll pick up [noun]..."* / *"Can you pick [me/it] up...?"*
- **When to use:** Logistics (rides, packages), skills (languages, trends), or restarting tasks.

#### 2. Look into (Inseparable)
*Meaning: Investigate, examine, research.*
- **Pattern:** `Look into [noun/gerund]` — **Never separate.** (❌ *Look it into*)
- **Spoken Formula:** *"I’ll look into [issue/possibility/complaint]."*
- **When to use:** Promising action without committing to a solution yet. Standard professional "holding pattern" phrase.

#### 3. Carry out (Separable)
*Meaning: Execute, perform, complete a task/plan/order.*
- **Pattern:** `Carry [plan/task] out` / `Carry out [plan/task]`
- **Spoken Formula:** *"We need to carry out [the audit/the plan]..."* / *"Who carried out [the test]?"*
- **When to use:** Formal execution of procedures, audits, surveys, or orders. Sounds more precise than "do."

#### 4. Turn out (Inseparable — usually followed by *to be* or *that*)
*Meaning: End up; result unexpectedly.*
- **Pattern:** `Turn out [to be / that]...`
- **Spoken Formula:** *"It turned out [to be easy / that she was right]."*
- **When to use:** Retelling stories, explaining outcomes, managing expectations. **Crucial for narrative flow.**

#### 5. Point out (Separable)
*Meaning: Draw attention to; indicate.*
- **Pattern:** `Point [fact/error/thing] out` / `Point out [fact/error/thing]`
- **Spoken Formula:** *"I’d like to point out [that...]"* / *"He pointed out [the error]."*
- **When to use:** Meetings, feedback, directions. Polite, professional way to correct or highlight without aggression.

## 3. Natural Spoken Examples & Dialogue

### "How Not to Say It" vs. "How a Clear Speaker Says It"

| Situation | ❌ Stiff / Unnatural (Overly Formal) | ✅ Clear Speaker (Natural Phrasal Verb) | Why the ✅ wins |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **1. Task Delegation** | "Please **execute** the new marketing strategy by Friday." | "Please **carry out** the new marketing strategy by Friday." | *Carry out* collocates naturally with *strategy/plan/order*; *execute* sounds military/legal. |
| **2. Problem Solving** | "I will **investigate** the server issue immediately." | "I’ll **look into** the server issue right away." | *Look into* is the standard spoken promise of action. *Investigate* implies a formal inquiry. |
| **3. Sharing a Result** | "The event **resulted in** a huge success." | "The event **turned out** to be a huge success." | *Turned out* conveys the *narrative journey* (we didn't know -> now we know). |
| **4. Giving Feedback** | "I want to **indicate** a discrepancy in the budget." | "I just want to **point out** a discrepancy in the budget." | *Point out* is collaborative; *indicate* is cold/clinical. |
| **5. Logistics/Learning** | "I **acquired** some Spanish while traveling." | "I **picked up** some Spanish while traveling." | *Picked up* implies organic, effortless learning. *Acquired* is academic. |

### 2-Person Dialogue Snippets

**Snippet A: Project Check-in (Carry out / Look into / Point out)**
> **Manager:** "Did we **carry out** the client survey last week?"
> **Lead:** "Yes, but the response rate was low. I’ll **look into** why the email bounced."
> **Manager:** "Good. Also, **point out** the new deadline to the team when you send the reminder."

**Snippet B: Casual Catch-up (Pick up / Turn out)**
> **Alex:** "How was the conference?"
> **Sam:** "Exhausting, but I **picked up** some great AI workflow tips."
> **Alex:** "Nice. Did the keynote happen?"
> **Sam:** "Yeah. The speaker was late, but it **turned out** to be the best session of the day."

**Snippet C: Clarifying a Misunderstanding (Point out / Turn out)**
> **Client:** "You charged me for the premium tier."
> **Rep:** "Ah, let me **point out** Section 4 — that covers the setup fee."
> **Client:** "Oh. Well, that **turns out** to be cheaper than I thought."

**Snippet D: Rescheduling (Pick up / Carry out)**
> **Colleague:** "Can we **pick up** the budget review at 3 PM? I have a dentist appointment."
> **You:** "Sure. I’ll **carry out** the variance analysis in the meantime so we’re ready."

**Snippet E: Troubleshooting (Look into / Turn out)**
> **Dev:** "The login page is throwing a 500 error."
> **Lead:** "I’ll **look into** the logs now."
> *(5 mins later)*
> **Lead:** "It **turned out** to be a cache issue. Cleared it; we’re live."

## 4. Verbal Practice & Content Drills

### Drill 1: 1-Minute Speaking Prompt
**Topic:** *"A Recent Surprise or Lesson Learned"*
**Instructions:** Record yourself speaking for 60 seconds. You **must** use at least **3 of the 5 phrasal verbs** (Pick up, Look into, Carry out, Turn out, Point out).
**Focus:** Narrative flow. Use *Turn out* for the punchline. Use *Pick up* for a skill/habit. Use *Look into/Carry out* for actions taken.

> *Example Start:* "Last month I tried to **carry out** a new morning routine. I **picked up** a habit of journaling... but it **turned out** to be too time-consuming..."

---

### Drill 2: Workplace Scenario — The "Scope Creep" Meeting
**Task:** You are a Project Manager. A stakeholder asks for a new feature *today*. You cannot do it, but you don't want to say "No" bluntly.
**Scenario:** Stakeholder: *"Can we add the dark-mode toggle before Friday launch?"*
**Model Spoken Response:**
> "I appreciate the ask. I need to **point out** that the code freeze started Monday. However, I can **look into** the effort required for a post-launch patch. If it’s low lift, we’ll **carry out** the deployment in the first sprint next month. It **turned out** well last time we phased a feature like this."

**Key Moves:** *Point out* (constraint) → *Look into* (action) → *Carry out* (future execution) → *Turn out* (precedent/reassurance).

---

### Drill 3: Real-Life Scenario — The "Airport/Errands" Logistics
**Task:** Coordinating a pickup/drop-off via phone or voice note.
**Scenario:** You are texting/calling a partner/friend about grocery runs and a package.
**Model Spoken Response:**
> "Hey, I’m heading out. I’ll **pick up** the dry cleaning and that Amazon package from the locker. Can you **pick up** milk and eggs? Also, the car is making a weird noise — I’ll **look into** it this weekend. It **turned out** the mechanic was booked solid last week, so I’ll book early this time."

**Key Moves:** *Pick up* (x2, physical collection) → *Look into* (problem solving) → *Turn out* (explaining past context).

## 5. Daily Implementation Action

**The "Meeting Swap" Challenge:**
In your next meeting, Slack update, or conversation today, **force yourself to replace one formal verb with its phrasal partner.**
1.  Swap **"Investigate / Check"** → **"Look into"**
2.  Swap **"Execute / Do / Perform"** → **"Carry out"**
3.  Swap **"End up / Result"** → **"Turn out"**
4.  Swap **"Mention / Highlight / Show"** → **"Point out"**
5.  Swap **"Learn / Get / Collect"** → **"Pick up"**

**Accountability:** Write down the **exact sentence** you said (or typed) where you made the swap. Say it out loud one more time to lock in the muscle memory.
